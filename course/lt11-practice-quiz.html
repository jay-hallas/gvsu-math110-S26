    Worksheet Learning Target 11: Solving Exponential and Logarithmic Equations
    

    Objectives

    • Solve equations and word situations involving exponential, logarithmic, and power functions symbolically and with technology.
      🔗
    🔗

    1. Solving with Base \(e\).

    Consider the equation \(4e^{2x} - 5 = 31\text{.}\)
    🔗

    (a)

    Solve for \(x\text{.}\) Give the exact answer first, then round to three decimal places.
    🔗
    Answer.
    Add \(5\) to both sides to get \(4e^{2x} = 36\text{,}\) then divide by \(4\) so that \(e^{2x} = 9\text{.}\) Take the natural logarithm of both sides: \(\ln(e^{2x}) = \ln(9)\text{,}\) so \(2x = \ln(9)\text{.}\) The exact answer is \(x = \frac{\ln(9)}{2} \approx 1.099\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    (b)

    Explain why taking \(\ln\) of both sides is the useful move here, rather than \(\log\text{.}\)
    🔗
    Answer.
    The natural logarithm is the inverse of the base \(e\) exponential, so \(\ln(e^{2x})\) collapses directly to \(2x\text{.}\) Using \(\log\) would still work, but it would leave a \(\log(e)\) factor to divide out.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    2. Solving with Another Base.

    Consider the equation \(7 \cdot 3^{x+1} = 250\text{.}\)
    🔗

    (a)

    Solve for \(x\text{.}\) Round to three decimal places.
    🔗
    Answer.
    Divide both sides by \(7\) to isolate the exponential: \(3^{x+1} = \frac{250}{7}\text{.}\) Take \(\ln\) of both sides and use the power rule: \((x+1)\ln(3) = \ln\left(\frac{250}{7}\right)\text{.}\) Then \(x + 1 = \frac{\ln(250/7)}{\ln(3)} \approx 3.255\text{,}\) so \(x \approx 2.255\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    (b)

    Check your answer by graphing \(y = 7 \cdot 3^{x+1}\) and \(y = 250\) in Desmos and finding the intersection. Record what you see.
    🔗
    Answer.
    The curves cross at approximately \((2.255, 250)\text{,}\) which matches the algebraic solution.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    3. A Logarithmic Equation.

    Consider the equation \(\log_4(3x + 2) = 3\text{.}\)
    🔗

    (a)

    Solve for \(x\text{.}\) Give an exact answer.
    🔗
    Answer.
    Rewrite the equation in exponential form: \(4^3 = 3x + 2\text{,}\) so \(64 = 3x + 2\text{.}\) Subtract \(2\) to get \(3x = 62\text{,}\) so \(x = \frac{62}{3}\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    (b)

    Substitute your answer back into the original equation to confirm it works.
    🔗
    Answer.
    With \(x = \frac{62}{3}\text{,}\) the inside of the logarithm is \(3\left(\frac{62}{3}\right) + 2 = 64\text{,}\) and \(\log_4(64) = 3\) since \(4^3 = 64\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    4. A Natural Logarithmic Equation.

    Consider the equation \(6\ln(2x) - 5 = 13\text{.}\)
    🔗

    (a)

    Solve for \(x\text{.}\) Give the exact answer first, then round to three decimal places.
    🔗
    Answer.
    Add \(5\) to both sides to get \(6\ln(2x) = 18\text{,}\) then divide by \(6\) so that \(\ln(2x) = 3\text{.}\) Rewrite in exponential form: \(2x = e^3\text{.}\) The exact answer is \(x = \frac{e^3}{2} \approx 10.043\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    (b)

    Would a negative solution ever be acceptable for an equation of this type? Explain.
    🔗
    Answer.
    No. The input of a logarithm has to be positive, so any candidate solution that makes \(2x\) zero or negative has to be thrown out. Here \(x = \frac{e^3}{2}\) is positive, so it is valid.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    5. Vehicle Depreciation.

    A delivery van is purchased for \(\$27,500\text{.}\) Its value drops by \(13\%\) each year.
    🔗

    (a)

    Write a function \(V(t)\) for the value of the van after \(t\) years.
    🔗
    Answer.
    The initial value is \(27500\) and the decay factor is \(1 - 0.13 = 0.87\text{,}\) so \(V(t) = 27500(0.87)^t\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    (b)

    Find the value of the van after \(7\) years. Round to the nearest cent and explain what your answer means.
    🔗
    Answer.
    \(V(7) = 27500(0.87)^7 \approx 10374.51\text{.}\) After \(7\) years the van is worth about \(\$10,374.51\text{,}\) which is a little over a third of what the company paid for it.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    (c)

    The company plans to replace the van once it is worth less than \(\$8,000\text{.}\) Set up and solve an equation to find when that happens. Round to two decimal places.
    🔗
    Answer.
    Set \(8000 = 27500(0.87)^t\text{,}\) so \((0.87)^t = \frac{8000}{27500} \approx 0.290909\text{.}\) Take \(\ln\) of both sides: \(t\ln(0.87) = \ln(0.290909)\text{,}\) so \(t = \frac{\ln(0.290909)}{\ln(0.87)} \approx 8.86\) years. The van drops below \(\$8,000\) partway through its ninth year.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    6. Reaching a Savings Goal.

    A family deposits \(\$6,200\) into an account that earns \(4.8\%\) annual interest, compounded monthly. They want the balance to reach \(\$9,000\text{.}\)
    🔗

    (a)

    Write the compound interest equation you would need to solve. Identify \(P\text{,}\) \(r\text{,}\) and \(n\text{.}\)
    🔗
    Answer.
    Here \(P = 6200\text{,}\) \(r = 0.048\text{,}\) and \(n = 12\text{.}\) The equation is \(9000 = 6200\left(1 + \frac{0.048}{12}\right)^{12t}\text{,}\) which simplifies to \(9000 = 6200(1.004)^{12t}\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    (b)

    Solve for \(t\text{.}\) Round to two decimal places.
    🔗
    Answer.
    Divide both sides by \(6200\text{:}\) \((1.004)^{12t} = \frac{9000}{6200} \approx 1.451613\text{.}\) Take \(\ln\) of both sides and use the power rule: \(12t\ln(1.004) = \ln(1.451613)\text{.}\) Then \(t = \frac{\ln(1.451613)}{12\ln(1.004)} \approx 7.78\) years.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    (c)

    If the account compounded quarterly instead of monthly, would you expect the answer to be larger or smaller? Explain your reasoning without doing the calculation.
    🔗
    Answer.
    Larger. Compounding less often means interest is added to the balance fewer times per year, so the money grows a bit more slowly and the family would have to wait longer to reach \(\$9,000\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
