    Worksheet Activity: Linear Versus Exponential Patterns
    

    For each of the following patterns, complete the steps below.
    • Draw or sketch Case 4 and Case 5 (if the number of shapes gets too large, just sketch the outline and write the dimensions).
      🔗
      🔗
    • Complete the table.
      🔗
      🔗
    • Determine whether the growth is linear, exponential, or neither and explain how you know.
      🔗
      🔗
    • If linear or exponential, find an equation for the number of shapes (\(y\)) in terms of the case number (\(n\)).
      🔗
      🔗
    • Check that your equation works by plugging in some cases from the table.
      🔗
      🔗
    🔗

    (a)
    Complete the table.

    24; 48.

    🔗
    🔗

    Is the growth linear, exponential, or neither? Circle one and explain how you know.

    Equation (if applicable):

    5. Concept Check: Classifying from a Table.

    For each table below, fill in the missing values, classify the pattern as linear, exponential, or neither, and write an equation if one exists.
    🔗
    (a)
    Table A
    🔗
    \(n\) 1 2 3 4 5
    \(y\) 4 12 36
    Type: \(\quad\) Equation:
    🔗
    Answer.
    Exponential growth (ratio 3). \(y = 4 \cdot 3^{n-1}\text{;}\) values 108 and 324.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    (b)
    Table B
    🔗
    \(n\) 1 2 3 4 5
    \(y\) 30 24 18
    Type: \(\quad\) Equation:
    🔗
    Answer.
    Linear decreasing (difference \(-6\)). \(y = -6n + 36\text{;}\) values 12 and 6.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    (c)
    Table C
    🔗
    \(n\) 1 2 3 4 5
    \(y\) 2 5 10
    Type: \(\quad\) Equation:
    🔗
    Answer.
    Neither (quadratic). \(y = n^2 + 1\text{;}\) values 17 and 26.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    (d)
    Table D
    🔗
    \(n\) 1 2 3 4 5
    \(y\) 96 48 24
    Type: \(\quad\) Equation:
    🔗
    Answer.
    Exponential decay (ratio \(\tfrac{1}{2}\)). \(y = 96 \cdot \left(\tfrac{1}{2}\right)^{n-1}\text{;}\) values 12 and 6.
