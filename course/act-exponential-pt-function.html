    Subsection Finding Exponential Functions Between Two Points

    Worksheet Exponential Equations from Two Points
    

    An exponential model has the form \(f(x) = ab^x\text{,}\) where \(a\) is the initial value and \(b\) is the growth or decay factor. This activity looks at two situations: one where you’re given the \(y\)-intercept outright, and one where you’re not.
    🔗
    Case 1: \(y\)-intercept given
    🔗

    Note 19. Finding \(a\) and \(b\) when the \(y\)-intercept is known.

    If one of your points is the \(y\)-intercept \((0,a)\text{,}\) you already know \(a\text{.}\) Just substitute the second point into \(y = ab^x\) and solve for \(b\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗

    Note 20. Worked Example.

    A population starts at \(5\) (at \(x=0\)) and grows to \(45\) by \(x=2\text{.}\) Find the exponential equation.
    🔗
    Points: \((0,5)\) and \((2,45)\text{.}\)
    🔗
    Step 1, find \(a\text{:}\) Since \((0,5)\) is the \(y\)-intercept, \(a=5\text{.}\)
    🔗
    Step 2, find \(b\text{:}\) Substitute the other point into \(y = 5b^x\text{:}\)
    🔗
    \begin{equation*} 45 \amp = 5b^2 \\ 9 \amp = b^2 \\ b \amp = 3 \end{equation*}
    🔗
    Step 3, write the equation:
    \begin{equation*} y = 5(3)^x \end{equation*}
    🔗
    🔗

    1.

    A quantity has \(y\)-intercept \((0,4)\) and also passes through \((3,32)\text{.}\) Find the exponential equation \(y = ab^x\) that models this data.
    🔗
    Answer.
    \(a = 4\text{,}\) and substituting \((3,32)\) gives \(32 = 4b^3\text{,}\) so \(8 = b^3\) and \(b = 2\text{.}\) The equation is \(y = 4(2)^x\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    2.

    A quantity has \(y\)-intercept \((0,100)\) and also passes through \((2,25)\text{.}\) Find the exponential equation \(y = ab^x\) that models this data.
    🔗
    Answer.
    \(a = 100\text{,}\) and substituting \((2,25)\) gives \(25 = 100b^2\text{,}\) so \(0.25 = b^2\) and \(b = 0.5\text{.}\) The equation is \(y = 100(0.5)^x\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    Case 2: \(y\)-intercept not given
    🔗

    Note 21. Finding \(a\) and \(b\) when the \(y\)-intercept is NOT known.

    If neither point is the \(y\)-intercept, set up two equations: \(y_1 = ab^{x_1}\) and \(y_2 = ab^{x_2}\text{.}\)
    🔗
    Divide the equations to eliminate \(a\text{:}\) \(\dfrac{y_2}{y_1} = b^{\,x_2 - x_1}\text{.}\) Solve for \(b\text{.}\)
    🔗
    Substitute \(b\) back into either original equation to solve for \(a\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗

    Note 22. Worked Example.

    A quantity passes through \((1,6)\) and \((3,54)\text{.}\) Find the exponential equation.
    🔗
    Step 1, divide to find \(b\text{:}\)
    🔗
    \begin{equation*} \frac{y_2}{y_1} \amp = \frac{54}{6} = 9, \qquad \text{and} \qquad \frac{y_2}{y_1} = b^{3-1} = b^2 \\ 9 \amp = b^2 \\ b \amp = 3 \end{equation*}
    🔗
    Step 2, substitute back to find \(a\text{:}\) Using \((1,6)\) in \(y = ab^x\text{:}\)
    🔗
    \begin{equation*} 6 \amp = a(3)^1 \\ a \amp = 2 \end{equation*}
    🔗
    Step 3, write the equation:
    \begin{equation*} y = 2(3)^x \end{equation*}
    🔗
    🔗

    3.

    A quantity passes through \((2,20)\) and \((4,80)\text{.}\) Find the exponential equation \(y = ab^x\) that models this data.
    🔗
    Answer.
    Dividing gives \(\frac{80}{20} = b^{4-2}\text{,}\) so \(4 = b^2\) and \(b = 2\text{.}\) Using \((2,20)\) in \(y = ab^x\) gives \(20 = a(2)^2\text{,}\) so \(a = 5\text{.}\) The equation is \(y = 5(2)^x\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    4.

    A quantity passes through \((1,40)\) and \((3,10)\text{.}\) Find the exponential equation \(y = ab^x\) that models this data.
    🔗
    Answer.
    Dividing gives \(\frac{10}{40} = b^{3-1}\text{,}\) so \(0.25 = b^2\) and \(b = 0.5\text{.}\) Using \((1,40)\) in \(y = ab^x\) gives \(40 = a(0.5)^1\text{,}\) so \(a = 80\text{.}\) The equation is \(y = 80(0.5)^x\text{.}\)
    🔗
