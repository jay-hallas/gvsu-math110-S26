    Worksheet Learning Target 13: Quadratic Forms and Graphs
    

    Objectives

    • I can identify the horizontal intercepts, vertical intercept, and vertex of a quadratic function from its graph.
      🔗
    • I can write the equation of a quadratic function in factored, vertex, and general forms.
      🔗
    • I can evaluate \(f(a)\) and solve for \(x\) given \(f(x) = a\) using multiple representations.
      🔗
    🔗

    1. Graph A.

    The graph below represents a quadratic function \(f\text{.}\) The horizontal intercepts, vertical intercept, and vertex all occur at integer coordinates.
    🔗

    (a)

    Identify the horizontal intercepts, vertical intercept, and vertex of \(f\text{.}\) Does the parabola open upward or downward, and what does that tell you about the sign of \(a\text{?}\)
    🔗
    Answer.
    The horizontal intercepts are \((-2, 0)\) and \((4, 0)\text{.}\) The vertical intercept is \((0, -8)\text{.}\) The vertex is \((1, -9)\text{.}\) The parabola opens upward, which means the leading coefficient \(a\) must be positive.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    (b)

    Write \(f(x)\) in factored form. Use a point from the graph to solve for \(a\text{.}\)
    🔗
    Answer.
    Using the horizontal intercepts, the factored form is \(f(x) = a(x+2)(x-4)\text{.}\) Substituting the vertical intercept \((0, -8)\) gives \(-8 = a(0+2)(0-4)\text{,}\) which simplifies to \(-8 = -8a\text{,}\) so \(a = 1\text{.}\) The factored form is \(f(x) = (x+2)(x-4)\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    (c)

    Write \(f(x)\) in vertex form. Verify that your value of \(a\) matches what you found previously.
    🔗
    Answer.
    Using the vertex \((1, -9)\text{,}\) the vertex form is \(f(x) = a(x-1)^2 - 9\text{.}\) Since \(a = 1\) from the previous step, the equation is \(f(x) = (x-1)^2 - 9\text{.}\) We can verify this with the point \((4, 0)\text{:}\) \((4-1)^2 - 9 = 9 - 9 = 0\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    (d)

    Expand either of your previous answers to write \(f(x)\) in general form.
    🔗
    Answer.
    Expanding the factored form: \(f(x) = (x+2)(x-4) = x^2 - 4x + 2x - 8 = x^2 - 2x - 8\text{.}\) (Expanding the vertex form \((x-1)^2 - 9\) yields \(x^2 - 2x + 1 - 9\text{,}\) which also simplifies to \(x^2 - 2x - 8\)).
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    (e)

    Evaluate \(f(5)\text{.}\) Show your work using one of your equations.
    🔗
    Answer.
    Using general form: \(f(5) = (5)^2 - 2(5) - 8 = 25 - 10 - 8 = 7\text{.}\) The point \((5, 7)\) would be on the graph.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    (f)

    Find all values of \(x\) such that \(f(x) = -5\text{.}\) Show your algebraic steps.
    🔗
    Answer.
    Set the general form equal to \(-5\text{:}\) \(x^2 - 2x - 8 = -5\text{.}\) Add \(5\) to both sides to get \(x^2 - 2x - 3 = 0\text{.}\) Factoring gives \((x-3)(x+1) = 0\text{.}\) The solutions are \(x = 3\) and \(x = -1\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    2. Graph B.

    The graph below represents a quadratic function \(g\text{.}\) The horizontal intercepts, vertical intercept, and vertex all occur at integer coordinates.
    🔗

    (a)

    Identify the horizontal intercepts, vertical intercept, and vertex of \(g\text{.}\) Does the parabola open upward or downward, and what does that tell you about the sign of \(a\text{?}\)
    🔗
    Answer.
    The horizontal intercepts are \((-3, 0)\) and \((1, 0)\text{.}\) The vertical intercept is \((0, 6)\text{.}\) The vertex is \((-1, 8)\text{.}\) The parabola opens downward, which means the leading coefficient \(a\) must be negative.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    (b)

    Write \(g(x)\) in factored form. Use the vertex to solve for \(a\text{.}\)
    🔗
    Answer.
    Using the horizontal intercepts, the factored form is \(g(x) = a(x+3)(x-1)\text{.}\) Substituting the vertex \((-1, 8)\) gives \(8 = a(-1+3)(-1-1)\text{,}\) which simplifies to \(8 = a(2)(-2) = -4a\text{.}\) Dividing by \(-4\) yields \(a = -2\text{.}\) The factored form is \(g(x) = -2(x+3)(x-1)\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    (c)

    Write \(g(x)\) in vertex form. Use one of the horizontal intercepts to solve for \(a\text{.}\)
    🔗
    Answer.
    Using the vertex \((-1, 8)\text{,}\) the vertex form is \(g(x) = a(x+1)^2 + 8\text{.}\) Substituting the intercept \((1, 0)\) gives \(0 = a(1+1)^2 + 8\text{,}\) which simplifies to \(0 = 4a + 8\text{.}\) Solving for \(a\) gives \(4a = -8\text{,}\) so \(a = -2\text{.}\) The vertex form is \(g(x) = -2(x+1)^2 + 8\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    (d)

    Expand one of your answers above to write \(g(x)\) in general form.
    🔗
    Answer.
    Expanding the factored form: \(g(x) = -2(x^2 + 2x - 3) = -2x^2 - 4x + 6\text{.}\) (Note that the constant term \(6\) correctly matches the vertical intercept \((0, 6)\)).
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    (e)

    Evaluate \(g(-4)\text{.}\) Show your work.
    🔗
    Answer.
    Using factored form: \(g(-4) = -2(-4+3)(-4-1) = -2(-1)(-5) = -10\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    (f)

    Find all values of \(x\) such that \(g(x) = 6\text{.}\) Show your algebraic steps.
    🔗
    Answer.
    Set the general form equal to \(6\text{:}\) \(-2x^2 - 4x + 6 = 6\text{.}\) Subtracting \(6\) from both sides gives \(-2x^2 - 4x = 0\text{.}\) Factoring out a \(-2x\) gives \(-2x(x + 2) = 0\text{.}\) The solutions are \(x = 0\) and \(x = -2\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
