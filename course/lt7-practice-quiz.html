    Worksheet Learning Target 7: Exponential Patterns
    

    Objectives

    • I can recognize and identify key characteristics of an exponential pattern represented in a table, graph, equation or application.
      🔗
    • I can translate between different representations.
      🔗
    🔗

    1. Block Pattern.

    Consider the following pattern of blocks.
    🔗

    (a)

    How many shapes will be in Case 5?
    🔗
    Answer.
    The counts for the first four cases are \(4, 7, 10, 13\) . Continuing the pattern, Case 5 will have \(16\) blocks.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    (b)

    Is the pattern linear, exponential, or neither? Explain how you know.
    🔗
    Answer.
    The pattern is linear. The number of blocks increases by a constant added amount ( \(+3\) blocks) for each new case, which means it has a constant rate of change.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    (c)

    If the pattern is linear or exponential, find an equation that gives the number of shapes \(y\) at case number \(x\) .
    🔗
    Answer.
    The rate of change (slope) is \(3\) . Working backward, Case 0 would have \(4 - 3 = 1\) block, so the y-intercept is \(1\) . The equation is \(y = 3x + 1\) .
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    2. Circle Pattern.

    Consider the following pattern of circles.
    🔗

    (a)

    How many shapes will be in Case 5?
    🔗
    Answer.
    The counts are \(2, 4, 8, 16\) . The number of circles doubles each time, so Case 5 will have \(32\) circles.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    (b)

    Is the pattern linear, exponential, or neither? Explain how you know.
    🔗
    Answer.
    The pattern is exponential. The number of shapes is multiplied by a constant factor of \(2\) from one case to the next.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    (c)

    If the pattern is linear or exponential, find an equation that gives the number of shapes \(y\) at case number \(x\) .
    🔗
    Answer.
    The growth factor is \(2\) . Working backward, Case 0 would have \(2 \div 2 = 1\) circle. The initial value is \(1\) . The equation is \(y = 1 \cdot 2^x\) or simply \(y = 2^x\) .
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    3. Square Pattern.

    Consider the following pattern of squares.
    🔗

    (a)

    How many shapes will be in Case 5?
    🔗
    Answer.
    The counts are \(1, 4, 9, 16\) . The squares are arranged in grids of \(1 \times 1\text{,}\) \(2 \times 2\text{,}\) \(3 \times 3\text{,}\) and \(4 \times 4\) . Case 5 will be a \(5 \times 5\) grid, which means it will have \(25\) squares.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    (b)

    Is the pattern linear, exponential, or neither? Explain how you know.
    🔗
    Answer.
    The pattern is neither linear nor exponential. The number of shapes does not grow by adding a constant amount (the differences are \(+3, +5, +7\) ), and it does not grow by multiplying by a constant factor.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    (c)

    If the pattern is linear or exponential, find an equation that gives the number of shapes \(y\) at case number \(x\) .
    🔗
    Answer.
    Not applicable. Since the pattern is neither linear nor exponential, an equation of the form \(y = mx+b\) or \(y = a \cdot b^x\) cannot be used.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    4. Triangle Pattern.

    Consider the following pattern of triangles.
    🔗

    (a)

    How many shapes will be in Case 5?
    🔗
    Answer.
    The counts are \(3, 9, 27, 81\) . The number of triangles is multiplied by 3 each step, so Case 5 will have \(243\) triangles.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    (b)

    Is the pattern linear, exponential, or neither? Explain how you know.
    🔗
    Answer.
    The pattern is exponential. The count is multiplying by a constant growth factor of \(3\) from each case to the next.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    (c)

    If the pattern is linear or exponential, find an equation that gives the number of shapes \(y\) at case number \(x\) .
    🔗
    Answer.
    The growth factor is \(3\) . Working backward, Case 0 would have \(3 \div 3 = 1\) triangle. The initial value is \(1\) . The equation is \(y = 1 \cdot 3^x\) or \(y = 3^x\) .
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    5. Exponential Table.

    The following table represents an exponential relationship. Find an equation of the form \(y = a \cdot b^x\) that models the data.
    🔗
    \(x\) \(0\) \(1\) \(2\) \(3\)
    \(y\) \(2\) \(10\) \(50\) \(250\)
    Answer.
    The y-intercept (when \(x=0\) ) is \(2\) , so the initial value is \(a = 2\) . Checking the change from term to term, \(10 \div 2 = 5\) and \(50 \div 10 = 5\) . The constant growth factor is \(b = 5\) . The equation is \(y = 2 \cdot 5^x\) .
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    6. Exponential Graph.

    Given the exponential graph below, find an equation of the form \(y = a \cdot b^x\) that matches it.
    🔗
    (The points \((0,4)\text{,}\) \((1,2)\text{,}\) and \((2,1)\) are marked to help you.)
    🔗
    Answer.
    The graph crosses the y-axis at \((0,4)\) , so the initial value is \(a = 4\) . From \(x=0\) to \(x=1\) , the y-value changes from \(4\) to \(2\) . Because \(2 \div 4 = 0.5\) (or \(\frac{1}{2}\) ), the multiplier is \(b = 0.5\) . The equation is \(y = 4 \cdot (0.5)^x\) .
