    Worksheet Learning Target 9: Exponential Applications
    

    Objectives

    • Given an exponential word situation (half-life, compound interest, depreciation, or simple growth/decay), make an equation.
      🔗
    • Identify and explain the meaning of the growth/decay rate and the y-intercept in the context of the problem.
      🔗
    • Evaluate \(f(a)\) and explain its meaning, find \(x\) given \(f(a)\) and explain its meaning, and determine what inputs make sense.
      🔗
    🔗

    1. Caffeine Elimination.

    A person drinks a large coffee containing \(150\) milligrams of caffeine. The amount of caffeine in their body decreases by \(14\%\) each hour.
    🔗

    (a)

    Write an exponential function \(C(t)\) to model the amount of caffeine remaining in the body after \(t\) hours.
    🔗
    Answer.
    The initial amount is \(150\) and the decay factor is \(1 - 0.14 = 0.86\text{.}\) The equation is \(C(t) = 150(0.86)^t\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    (b)

    Identify the y-intercept and the decay rate. Explain their meanings in the context of this situation.
    🔗
    Answer.
    The y-intercept is \(150\text{,}\) which represents the initial \(150\) mg of caffeine in the body at the moment the coffee is consumed (\(t=0\)). The decay rate is \(14\%\text{,}\) meaning the body metabolizes and eliminates \(14\%\) of the remaining caffeine every hour.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    (c)

    Find \(C(5)\) and explain its meaning in context. Round to the nearest tenth.
    🔗
    Answer.
    \(C(5) = 150(0.86)^5 \approx 70.6\text{.}\) This means there are approximately \(70.6\) milligrams of caffeine left in the person’s body \(5\) hours after drinking the coffee.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    (d)

    Set up an equation to find when the caffeine level reaches \(20\) mg. Explain what the solution to this equation represents. (You do not need to solve it).
    🔗
    Answer.
    \(20 = 150(0.86)^t\text{.}\) The solution \(t\) represents the exact number of hours it will take for the caffeine in the body to drop to \(20\) milligrams.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    (e)

    What inputs for \(t\) make sense in this context?
    🔗
    Answer.
    Time cannot be negative, so \(t \ge 0\text{.}\) The inputs make sense for positive values of \(t\) until the caffeine approaches zero and is effectively eliminated from the bloodstream.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    2. Viral Video Growth.

    A new video is uploaded to a channel and initially has \(400\) views. For the first week it is released, the total number of views grows by \(18\%\) every hour.
    🔗

    (a)

    Write an exponential function \(V(t)\) to model the total number of views after \(t\) hours.
    🔗
    Answer.
    The initial amount is \(400\) and the growth factor is \(1 + 0.18 = 1.18\text{.}\) The equation is \(V(t) = 400(1.18)^t\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    (b)

    Find \(V(24)\) and explain its meaning in context. Round to the nearest whole number.
    🔗
    Answer.
    \(V(24) = 400(1.18)^{24} \approx 21255\text{.}\) This means that \(24\) hours (one full day) after the video was uploaded, it will have approximately \(21,255\) total views.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    (c)

    What domain of inputs for \(t\) makes sense based on the problem description?
    🔗
    Answer.
    The problem states the model applies for "the first week". A week has \(24 \times 7 = 168\) hours. Therefore, the valid inputs are \(0 \le t \le 168\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    3. AI Assistant Reliability.

    Researchers are testing a new AI assistant. As a simplified model, they assume the probability that a response is entirely correct starts at \(95\%\) (or \(0.95\)) and decreases by \(8\%\) for every additional \(500\) words generated in a single session.
    🔗

    (a)

    Write an exponential function \(P(w)\) to model the probability of a correct response based on \(w\text{,}\) the number of words generated. (Hint: Use \(\frac{w}{500}\) in your exponent to scale the words into \(500\)-word blocks).
    🔗
    Answer.
    The initial probability is \(0.95\text{.}\) The decay factor is \(1 - 0.08 = 0.92\text{.}\) Because this decay happens every \(500\) words, the exponent is \(\frac{w}{500}\text{.}\) The equation is \(P(w) = 0.95(0.92)^{w/500}\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    (b)

    Evaluate \(P(2000)\) and explain what it means in context.
    🔗
    Answer.
    \(P(2000) = 0.95(0.92)^{2000/500} = 0.95(0.92)^4 \approx 0.68\text{.}\) This means that if the AI generates a \(2000\)-word response, there is approximately a \(68\%\) probability that the response is entirely correct.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    4. Annual Compound Interest.

    An investor deposits \(\$5,000\) into a retirement account that earns \(6\%\) annual interest, compounded yearly.
    🔗

    (a)

    Write an equation for the balance \(A(t)\) after \(t\) years. Then, identify the y-intercept and explain its meaning.
    🔗
    Answer.
    The equation is \(A(t) = 5000(1.06)^t\text{.}\) The y-intercept is \(5000\text{,}\) which represents the initial principal deposited into the account at year \(0\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    (b)

    Evaluate \(A(10)\) and explain its meaning. (Round to the nearest cent).
    🔗
    Answer.
    \(A(10) = 5000(1.06)^{10} \approx 8954.24\text{.}\) This means that after \(10\) years, the account balance will have grown to \(\$8,954.24\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    5. Monthly Compound Interest.

    Suppose instead the \(\$5,000\) is deposited into an account that earns \(6\%\) annual interest, but it is compounded monthly.
    🔗

    (a)

    Write an equation for the balance \(A(t)\) after \(t\) years. (Recall the formula \(A = P\left(1 + \frac{r}{n}\right)^{nt}\)).
    🔗
    Answer.
    Here, \(P = 5000\text{,}\) \(r = 0.06\text{,}\) and \(n = 12\) (since there are \(12\) months in a year). The equation is \(A(t) = 5000\left(1 + \frac{0.06}{12}\right)^{12t}\text{,}\) which simplifies to \(A(t) = 5000(1.005)^{12t}\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    (b)

    Set up an equation to find how long it takes for the account balance to reach \(\$10,000\text{.}\) What does this solution represent?
    🔗
    Answer.
    \(10000 = 5000(1.005)^{12t}\text{.}\) The solution \(t\) represents the number of years it will take for the initial investment to double in value.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    6. Daily Compound Interest.

    Finally, suppose the \(\$5,000\) is deposited into an account earning \(6\%\) annual interest, compounded daily (assume \(365\) days in a year).
    🔗

    (a)

    Write an equation for the balance \(A(t)\) after \(t\) years.
    🔗
    Answer.
    Here, \(n = 365\text{.}\) The equation is \(A(t) = 5000\left(1 + \frac{0.06}{365}\right)^{365t}\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    (b)

    Evaluate \(A(10)\) for this daily compounding account. How does it compare to the annual compounding from the earlier question?
    🔗
    Answer.
    \(A(10) = 5000\left(1 + \frac{0.06}{365}\right)^{365(10)} \approx 9110.14\text{.}\) After \(10\) years, the daily compounding account yields \(\$9,110.14\text{,}\) which is more than the \(\$8,954.24\) earned from annual compounding. More frequent compounding results in a higher final balance.
