    Worksheet Group Quiz 3: Exponential Functions
    

    Work together as a group. Each problem is worth 5 points.
    🔗

    Note 28.

    Explain your reasoning in complete sentences where asked.
    🔗
    Show your work for any calculations in as much detail as possible.
    🔗
    Since it is a team quiz, make sure you agree on your final answers; think of ways to double check your work for each problem.
    🔗
    Invite everyone to participate. Try to build as much understanding for each problem.

    1. Growth Pattern: Circles.

    Consider the visual pattern below, where the number of circles increases at each step.
    🔗
    
    Step 1 shows a single column of 3 dots. Step 2 shows two columns of 3 dots each, for 6 dots total. Step 3 shows four columns of 3 dots each, for 12 dots total.
    🔗

    (a)

    Is this growth pattern linear, exponential, or neither? Explain how you can tell by examining how the number of circles changes from one step to the next.
    🔗
    Solution.
    This pattern represents exponential growth. We can tell by counting the total number of circles at each step: Step 1 has 3 circles, Step 2 has 6 circles, and Step 3 has 12 circles. From one step to the next, the number of circles is multiplied by 2 (it doubles). Because the pattern grows by a constant multiplier (growth factor of 2) rather than by adding a constant amount, it is an exponential function.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    (b)

    Write a formula for the total number of circles, \(C\text{,}\) in Step \(n\text{.}\)
    🔗
    Solution.
    We know that an exponential model has the general form \(C(n) = a \cdot b^n\text{,}\) where \(b\) is the growth factor and \(a\) is the initial value (when \(n=0\)).
    🔗
    Since the number of circles doubles at each step, our growth factor is \(b = 2\text{.}\) To find the initial value \(a\) at Step 0, we can work backward from Step 1 by dividing by 2.
    🔗
    \begin{gather*} a = \frac{3}{2} = 1.5 \end{gather*}
    🔗
    Therefore, the formula for the total number of circles in Step \(n\) is
    🔗
    \begin{gather*} C(n) = 1.5 \cdot 2^n \end{gather*}
    🔗
    You can also write the formula starting directly from Step 1 as \(C(n) = 3 \cdot 2^{n-1}\text{.}\) Both expressions are mathematically equivalent and fully correct.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    2. Exponential Graph: Finding the Equation.

    An exponential function of the form \(f(x) = a \cdot b^x\) is shown on the graph below. Several integer input points are highlighted.
    🔗
    
    A coordinate grid with the \(x\)-axis from -2.5 to 3.5 and the \(y\)-axis from -0.5 to 15. An increasing curve is highlighted at three points: \((0,5)\text{,}\) \((1,8)\text{,}\) and \((2,12.8)\text{.}\)
    🔗

    (a)

    Identify the vertical intercept from the graph. What parameter (\(a\) or \(b\)) does this value give you?
    🔗
    Solution.
    The vertical intercept (or \(y\)-intercept) is the point where the graph crosses the vertical axis, which is at \((0, 5)\text{.}\) In the general exponential equation \(f(x) = a \cdot b^x\text{,}\) plugging in \(x=0\) gives \(f(0) = a \cdot b^0 = a\text{.}\) Therefore, the vertical intercept gives us the initial value parameter, \(a = 5\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    (b)

    Use the coordinates of the plotted points to find the growth factor \(b\text{.}\) Show your work or explain how you found it.
    🔗
    Solution.
    The growth factor \(b\) represents the ratio of outputs for any two consecutive integer inputs. Using the plotted points \((0, 5)\) and \((1, 8)\text{,}\) we can divide the output at \(x=1\) by the output at \(x=0\text{.}\)
    🔗
    \begin{gather*} b = \frac{f(1)}{f(0)} = \frac{8}{5} = 1.6 \end{gather*}
    🔗
    We can verify this constant multiplier by checking the next plotted point at \(x=2\text{.}\)
    🔗
    \begin{gather*} b = \frac{f(2)}{f(1)} = \frac{12.8}{8} = 1.6 \end{gather*}
    🔗
    The growth factor is \(b = 1.6\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    (c)

    Write the complete equation for the exponential function shown above.
    🔗
    Solution.
    Substituting our initial value \(a = 5\) and our growth factor \(b = 1.6\) into the form \(f(x) = a \cdot b^x\text{,}\) we get the complete function:
    🔗
    \begin{gather*} f(x) = 5 \cdot (1.6)^x \end{gather*}
    🔗
    🔗

    3. Exponential Decay: Delivery Van Depreciation.

    A new delivery van is purchased for \$42,000. As soon as it is driven off the lot, it begins depreciating in value. Each year, the van loses 15% of its value from the previous year. Let \(V(t)\) represent the value of the van (in dollars) \(t\) years after it was purchased.
    🔗

    (a)

    State the initial value and determine the annual decay factor \(b\) for this situation. Explain how you found the decay factor.
    🔗
    Solution.
    The initial value is the purchase price of the van, so \(a = 42{,}000\text{.}\)
    🔗
    To find the decay factor \(b\text{,}\) we start with 100% of the value (or 1) and subtract the annual rate of depreciation, \(r = 15\% = 0.15\text{.}\)
    🔗
    \begin{gather*} b = 1 - r = 1 - 0.15 = 0.85 \end{gather*}
    🔗
    This means that after losing 15% of its value each year, the van retains 85% of its value from the previous year.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    (b)

    Write a function \(V(t)\) that models the van’s value over time.
    🔗
    Solution.
    Using the exponential model \(V(t) = a \cdot b^t\) with our initial value \(a = 42{,}000\) and decay factor \(b = 0.85\text{,}\) the function is:
    🔗
    \begin{gather*} V(t) = 42{,}000(0.85)^t \end{gather*}
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    (c)

    Find \(V(6)\) and explain what this number tells you about the van. Round your final dollar value to the nearest cent.
    🔗
    Solution.
    To find \(V(6)\text{,}\) substitute \(t = 6\) years into our valuation function.
    🔗
    \begin{gather*} V(6) = 42{,}000(0.85)^6 \end{gather*}
    🔗
    Calculating \((0.85)^6 \approx 0.3771495\text{:}\)
    🔗
    \begin{gather*} V(6) \approx 42{,}000(0.3771495) \approx 15840.279 \end{gather*}
    🔗
    Rounding to the nearest cent gives \$15,840.28. This number tells us that 6 years after being purchased, the delivery van’s value will have depreciated to approximately \$15,840.28.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    4. Compound Interest: Certificate of Deposit.

    Suppose you deposit $4,800 into a certificate of deposit (CD) that pays an annual interest rate of 5.2%, compounded monthly. Let \(A(t)\) be the account balance after \(t\) years. Assume no extra deposits or withdrawals are made.
    🔗

    (a)

    State the compound interest formula, and list the specific numerical values you will use for the principal \(P\text{,}\) the annual interest rate \(r\text{,}\) and the number of compounding periods per year \(n\text{.}\)
    🔗
    Solution.
    The compound interest formula is given by:
    🔗
    \begin{gather*} A(t) = P\left(1 + \frac{r}{n}\right)^{nt} \end{gather*}
    🔗
    For this specific account, we identify the following parameters from the problem statement.
    🔗
    Principal (initial deposit): \(P = 4{,}800\text{.}\)
    🔗
    Annual interest rate (as a decimal): \(r = 0.052\text{.}\)
    🔗
    Compounding periods per year (monthly): \(n = 12\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    (b)

    Write the complete function \(A(t)\) for the balance in this account after \(t\) years.
    🔗
    Solution.
    Substituting our values for \(P\text{,}\) \(r\text{,}\) and \(n\) into the formula yields:
    🔗
    \begin{gather*} A(t) = 4{,}800\left(1 + \frac{0.052}{12}\right)^{12t} \end{gather*}
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    (c)

    How much money will be in the account after 8 years? Show your calculation and round your final answer to the nearest cent.
    🔗
    Solution.
    To find the balance after 8 years, evaluate \(A(8)\) by substituting \(t = 8\text{.}\)
    🔗
    \begin{gather*} A(8) = 4{,}800\left(1 + \frac{0.052}{12}\right)^{12(8)} = 4{,}800\left(1 + \frac{0.052}{12}\right)^{96} \end{gather*}
    🔗
    Step by step calculation:
    🔗
    Find the monthly interest rate: \(\frac{0.052}{12} \approx 0.00433333\text{.}\)
    🔗
    Add 1 to get the monthly growth factor: \(1 + \frac{0.052}{12} \approx 1.00433333\text{.}\)
    🔗
    Raise this growth factor to the 96th power (total number of compounding months in 8 years): \((1.00433333)^{96} \approx 1.5143325\text{.}\)
    🔗
    Multiply by the principal: \(4{,}800 \cdot 1.5143325 \approx 7268.796\text{.}\)
    🔗
    Rounding to the nearest cent, there will be \$7,268.80 in the account after 8 years.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    5. Building an Exponential Function from Two Points.

    Find an exponential function of the form \(f(x) = a \cdot b^x\) that passes through the points \((1,50)\) and \((3,2)\text{.}\)
    🔗

    (a)

    Use the two given coordinate points to set up a system of two equations involving the variables \(a\) and \(b\text{.}\)
    🔗
    Solution.
    Each point \((x, y)\) on the graph must satisfy the equation \(y = a \cdot b^x\text{.}\) Substituting each coordinate pair into the general exponential form gives us our system of equations.
    🔗
    \begin{align*} \text{Using point } (1, 50) \text{:} \quad 50 \amp = a \cdot b^1 \\ \text{Using point } (3, 2) \text{:} \quad 2 \amp = a \cdot b^3 \end{align*}
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    (b)

    Solve your system algebraically to find the exact values of \(a\) and \(b\text{.}\) Show each step of your work clearly.
    🔗
    Solution.
    A great strategy for solving a system of exponential equations is to divide the second equation by the first equation. This eliminates the initial value variable \(a\text{.}\)
    🔗
    \begin{gather*} \frac{2}{50} = \frac{a \cdot b^3}{a \cdot b^1} \end{gather*}
    🔗
    Simplify both sides of the equation.
    🔗
    \begin{gather*} \frac{1}{25} = b^2 \end{gather*}
    🔗
    To solve for \(b\text{,}\) take the square root of both sides. Since the base of an exponential function must be positive (\(b > 0\)), we take the positive root.
    🔗
    \begin{gather*} b = \sqrt{\frac{1}{25}} = \frac{1}{5} = 0.2 \end{gather*}
    🔗
    Now that we have the exact growth factor \(b = \frac{1}{5}\text{,}\) substitute it back into our simplest first equation (\(50 = a \cdot b\)) to solve for \(a\text{.}\)
    🔗
    \begin{gather*} 50 = a \cdot \left(\frac{1}{5}\right) \end{gather*}
    🔗
    Multiply both sides by 5.
    🔗
    \begin{gather*} a = 250 \end{gather*}
    🔗
    Thus, the exact values are \(a = 250\) and \(b = \frac{1}{5}\) (or 0.2).
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    (c)

    Write your final exponential function \(f(x) = a \cdot b^x\text{.}\)
    🔗
    Solution.
    Substituting \(a = 250\) and \(b = \frac{1}{5}\) into the general form, our exponential function is:
    🔗
    \begin{gather*} f(x) = 250\left(\frac{1}{5}\right)^x \quad \text{or} \quad f(x) = 250(0.2)^x \end{gather*}
    🔗
